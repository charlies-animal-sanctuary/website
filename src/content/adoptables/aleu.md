bio: |-
  Hi, I'm Aleu. I'm a 1.5-year-old 45lb Husky mix from the tundra, and these days I'm a professional recliner tester and lap dog. Yes, a full-grown Husky who thinks she's a Chihuahua. I don't see the problem.

  I wasn't lost on the tundra. I'd made a life for myself. I was a very pregnant stray living alongside a team of tethered sled dogs. I could wander wherever I wanted, but I always came back to sleep inside the circle of the lead dog's chain. He was the father of my puppies, and that small, limited circle was my spot.

  It took my rescuers three weeks to earn enough trust to catch me, and in the end, it took a whole deboned rotisserie chicken to convince me. I was too pregnant to run, so I rolled onto my back and offered my belly in submission.

  When I first arrived at my foster home, I was terrified. For the first five hours, I wouldn't even lie down in my kennel. After a day, my foster mom finally encouraged me into a play yard pen she'd set up for me. For days, whenever she entered the room, I tried to disappear into the wall. She started singing "Allo, Aleu" every time she came in, gently tossing a treat near me. It took a long time before I was brave enough to take one from her hand.

  What really helped was Mojo, the senior dog in my foster home. My foster mom brought a dog bed beside my pen, and Mojo would just lie there. His calm became my calm.

  Then I had my puppies, and everything changed. The dog who had wanted to disappear suddenly wanted her foster mom in the pen with her. All. The. Time. For weeks, she sat with me for an hour out of every two, 24 hours a day. If she left, I was too unsettled to feed my babies. If she stayed, I could relax enough to be their mom. And I was a wonderful momma.

  My puppies grew up, became part of the family crew, and eventually headed off to rescue to begin their own adventures. I stayed behind.

  These days, I'm a sweet, soulful girl who's learning what it means to be loved and to feel safe. My foster family has poured so much love into me, and I'm slowly discovering that the world doesn't have to be such a scary place.

  My biggest adventure of the day is putting on my harness and double leash to stand in the doorway, attached to our tie-out line, while I watch my foster mom take Mojo out front. I can choose to join them, but usually I prefer to stand beside the family Chihuahua and watch with curiosity. I like to think we're both saying, "No thanks, we're Chihuahuas. We'll stay here." As soon as I'm detached, I make a beeline for my recliner.

  I love snuggling on my foster mom's lap under a blanket, just like a Chihuahua. I've even tried climbing onto the back of her recliner to perch like a cat. Apparently, that's a little unusual for a full-grown Husky, but I'm just trying to fit in with my pack. I learn a lot by watching the other animals around me, and I want to be part of everything they do.

  I'm learning to sit, lie down, and go to my spot. There's a crate in our home that everyone takes turns using, but I usually prefer my recliner or one of the many dog beds scattered around the house. Sometimes you'll find me snuggled up in a pile with the other dogs. I'm also incredibly gentle when taking treats.

  I'm still figuring out a few things. I'm going through a slightly delayed adolescence and have recently discovered the exciting world of counter-surfing. I'm also learning about personal space, how strong I am, and the difference between playing with other dogs and playing with people. I'm making progress, but I'll need someone patient enough to keep teaching me.

  One thing I'm already wonderful at is making friends with other dogs. I've done beautifully with every single dog who's come through my foster home, whether they're my age, younger, older, tiny puppies, or other fosters. I would do best in a calm, patient home with other calm, sociable dogs who can help show me the way. I might even enjoy having a puppy foster or two in a spare room that I could check in on from time to time.

  Because I spent my early life roaming freely on the tundra, my foster family recommends using a double leash until I'm confident being out and about and my new people are confident in my recall. A secure tie-out line in the yard may also be a good idea rather than letting me loose, just in case my old instinct to bolt returns. After all, I was very hard to catch the first time!

  The biggest thing I need from my future family is patience. My foster family is the only family I've ever really known, and leaving them will be scary at first. I'll need time to build trust, feel safe, and form a bond with my new people. But all the love I've received here has taught me something important: I know what love feels like now. I've learned how to recognize it. I just need someone willing to give me the time to recognize it again.

  When the world feels too big, I retreat to my recliner, look up with pleading eyes, and roll over to offer my belly. It's the same thing I did that first day on the tundra, but now it means something completely different. I'm not offering my belly in submission anymore. I'm asking for love, because I've learned that I can trust the people around me.

  If you're looking for a gentle, soulful companion and have the patience to help me continue discovering this big, beautiful world in a safe and not-so-scary way, I would love the chance to become part of your family.

  I've come such a long way already, and I have so much love left to give.

  Allo, Aleu.
